# DM-003 — Market Heterogeneity and Asset-Selection Opportunity

**Category:** Direct Monetization  
**Status:** approved for research; not empirically validated  
**Priority:** 3

## Hypothesis
Periods of higher cross-sectional heterogeneity increase the dispersion of subsequent relative returns and may increase the economic opportunity available to a valid point-in-time asset-selection process.

## Economic mechanism
Instruments differ in sector exposures, fundamentals, liquidity, investor bases, and sensitivity to information. When common market movement is less dominant, instrument-specific developments may account for a larger share of return differences.

## Expected behavior and direction
Higher current cross-sectional dispersion is expected to be associated with greater future cross-sectional dispersion. This does not identify which assets will outperform. The asset-selection mechanism is a separate necessary component.

## Economic-value mechanism
If an independent selection process can rank future relative winners before outcomes occur, a more heterogeneous environment may offer greater scope for positive active return. Heterogeneity alone is not a tradable signal.

## Instruments and horizons
Initial scope: MOEX TOP50 PIT constituents. State windows: 20/60 sessions. Future dispersion/selection horizons: 5/20/60 sessions.

## Required data
PIT daily return panel and membership; cross-sectional dispersion measures; candidate ranking information available at formation; sector/size/liquidity controls; realistic execution costs for later portfolio tests.

## Candidate variables
Cross-sectional standard deviation, MAD, and IQR; share of positive/negative returns; median pairwise dependence; predeclared candidate rank dispersion and subsequent rank outcomes.

## Confounders
Universe size/composition, sector weights, outliers, market-wide volatility, unequal histories, and the quality of the selection signal itself.

## Applicability
Control for universe composition and number of eligible securities. The selection rule must be specified independently and evaluated on PIT cohorts. Never use future rank or future membership to define the formation-time opportunity set.

## Confirmation
- Current heterogeneity predicts greater future cross-sectional opportunity under a predeclared measure.
- The relationship persists across periods and matched universes.
- A selection process specified without lookahead earns incremental net return in high-heterogeneity states versus a suitable benchmark.

## Rejection
- Higher dispersion does not persist or does not translate into larger future relative-return differences.
- No available formation-time information can identify winners better than baseline.
- Apparent opportunity is explained by composition or market beta.
- Net value disappears after costs or is confined to a single period.

## EDA basis
EDA2 blocks 1, 3, 4, and 5 documented heterogeneous returns, pairwise dependence, and time-varying dispersion. They did not show that dispersion predicts future winners. This separates opportunity-set width from the ability to select assets.
